Causatives and Permissives.

How a subject causes or permits someone else to act.

Causatives and PermissivesSlide 1 of 9
Slide 1 of 9

Summary

The subject of a causative or permissive sentence causes or permits another person, the agent, to perform an action. The lesson names the subject, main verb, agent, causative verb, and object, then compares three ways to arrange them.

The examples use have my taxes done, have an accountant do my taxes, and get an accountant to do my taxes. The final slide lists common main verbs for each structure.

Important points

  • The main verb is the subject’s conjugated verb; the causative verb expresses the agent’s action.
  • Causative 1 places the object before a participle. The agent can follow by and may be omitted.
  • Causative 2 places the agent before the simple form of the causative verb.
  • Causative 3 places the agent before an infinitive with to.
  • Some main verbs appear in more than one structure; the final slide groups common verbs by pattern.
