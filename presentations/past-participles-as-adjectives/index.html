Past Participles as Adjectives.

How past participles and participle phrases can modify nouns.

Past Participles as AdjectivesSlide 1 of 9
Slide 1 of 9

Summary

Past Participles as Adjectives explains how a past participle can modify a noun and therefore function as an adjective. The presentation contrasts an ordinary adjective in a good report with the past participle in a written report.

It presents three patterns: a past participle by itself before a noun, an adverb followed by a past participle before a noun, and a participle phrase placed after the noun it describes. The examples also show participle phrases as reductions of subordinate clauses.

Important points

  • A past participle can function as an adjective when it modifies a noun.
  • A past participle used by itself comes before the noun, as in an organized person.
  • An adverb can come before a past participle, as in a highly respected company.
  • A participle phrase comes after the noun it describes, as in steaks cooked on a barbecue.
  • The presentation describes participle phrases as reductions of subordinate clauses.
  • Past participles functioning as adjectives follow the rules given for adjectives in the presentation.
