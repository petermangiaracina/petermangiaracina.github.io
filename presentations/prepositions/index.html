Prepositions in Context.

Stop choosing a word in isolation. Learn the phrase and the situation around it.

All levelsSlide 1 of 11
Slide 1 of 11

Synopsis

This presentation explains why prepositions are easier to learn in context than through direct translation. A single Spanish preposition such as en may correspond to at, in, or on in English, depending on the relationship being described and the phrase English speakers conventionally use.

The lesson distinguishes meaning-led choices from fixed combinations. It encourages students to learn a headword, its preposition, and its context as one unit, then strengthen that unit through noticing, chunking, contrasting, and retrieving.

Important points

  • Translate the entire scene or relationship, not one isolated word.
  • Learn useful chunks such as depend on, interested in, and responsible for.
  • Meaning can guide choices such as arrive at the station and arrive in Madrid.
  • Conventional combinations often have to be learned and remembered as complete phrases.
  • Use the four-part method: notice, chunk, contrast, and retrieve.
