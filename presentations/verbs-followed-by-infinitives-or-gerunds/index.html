Verbs Followed by Infinitives or Gerunds.

Which verbs take gerunds, infinitives, or either form, and when meaning changes.

Summary

Verbs Followed by Infinitives or Gerunds organizes common verb patterns into three groups. Some verbs are followed by a gerund, some are followed by an infinitive, and some can be followed by either form. The presentation emphasizes learning the pattern associated with each verb.

Several verbs accept a gerund or an infinitive with no change of meaning. Other verbs—including forget, remember, stop, go on, and try—change meaning according to the form that follows them.

Important points

  • A gerund uses the -ing form of the verb.
  • An infinitive uses to + verb.
  • Verbs such as enjoy, avoid, and suggest are followed by gerunds.
  • Verbs such as decide, hope, and promise are followed by infinitives.
  • Verbs such as begin, continue, and prefer can take either form without a change of meaning.
  • With some verbs, choosing the gerund or infinitive changes the meaning of the expression.
