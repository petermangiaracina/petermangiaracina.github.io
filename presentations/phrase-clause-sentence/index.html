Phrases, Clauses, and Sentences.

Recognize the parts of a sentence and how they fit together.

Slide 1 of 7

Synopsis

This presentation explains the difference between phrases, clauses, and sentences. It introduces prepositional, gerund, and infinitive phrases, then shows how main and subordinate clauses fit together. Examples progress from a sentence with one clause to sentences with several clauses.

The final diagrams break sentences into their grammatical parts. They show how phrases add information or act as a subject, and how a subordinate clause depends on a main clause. Recognizing these parts helps you understand how an English sentence is built.

Important points

  • A phrase is a group of words without its own subject and finite verb combination.
  • A basic clause normally contains a subject and a finite verb.
  • A main or independent clause can stand alone as a complete sentence.
  • A subordinate or dependent clause needs a main clause.
  • A sentence can contain one clause or several clauses, as well as phrases.
  • A gerund phrase can act as the subject: in “Driving at night is dangerous,” “Driving at night” is the subject and “is dangerous” is the predicate.
