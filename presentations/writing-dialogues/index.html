Writing Dialogues.

How to create, format, and perform a dialogue built around conflict and resolution.

Writing DialoguesSlide 1 of 12
Slide 1 of 12

Summary

Writing Dialogues treats a dialogue as a way to explore verbal interactions in a controlled environment. The central concepts are personal agendas, collaboration, negotiation, conflict, and conflict resolution. Conflict makes the situation harder to solve and creates a wider range of emotions and language.

The lesson also covers cast, settings, suggested situations, professional dialogue format, and the final writing and performance assignment. The dialogue should contain a conflict that the characters resolve through collaboration and negotiation.

Important points

  • Dialogues explore verbal interactions in a controlled environment.
  • Personal agendas can clash and create conflict.
  • Negotiation and collaboration resolve the conflict.
  • Possible characters include doctors, patients, nurses, drug representatives, and administrators.
  • Possible settings include meetings, telephone calls, interviews, patient visits, drug-trial discussions, and conference questions and answers.
  • A professional dialogue identifies location, description, characters, dialogue, stage directions, and character descriptions.
  • The assignment requires at least two pages and at least two characters.
  • The writer performs one character and directs the other actors.
