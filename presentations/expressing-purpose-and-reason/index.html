Expressing Purpose & Reason.

A practical guide to saying what you want to achieve and why things happen.

Synopsis

This lesson explains the difference between purpose, what someone wants to do or achieve, and reason, why something happens. It shows how English uses to + infinitive, in order to + infinitive, and so (that) + clause for purpose, while because + clause, because of + noun, and since + clause can express reason.

Important points

  • Use to + infinitive to express purpose.
  • In order to expresses the same purpose more explicitly.
  • Use for + noun, not for + gerund, to express purpose in these patterns.
  • So (that) is followed by a subject and verb.
  • Because is followed by a clause, while because of is followed by a noun.
  • Since can mean because, although it also has a separate time meaning.
